Diffie-Hellman, in paint

Alice and Bob agree a shared secret over a line anyone can listen on, without ever sending the secret. Mixing paint is easy; pulling it apart again is practically impossible, and that one-way step is the whole trick. Set each secret and step through.

These are agreed in the open before Alice and Bob start, so they are not secret: Eve knows p, g and the base paint too. Real systems reuse standard published values for p and g. Only each side's secret (a and b) stays private.

Mixing is one-way: easy to combine, practically impossible to pull back apart. Eve's best move is to combine the two public mixtures, but that just gives her the base paint twice over, a different colour from the shared secret:

Both land on gab mod p. Eve sees p, g, A and B, but to get the secret she needs a from A, the discrete logarithm problem. Here p = 23 is tiny, so she can just try every power (the Eve button above). Real Diffie-Hellman uses a p hundreds of digits long, where no known method is fast enough.
